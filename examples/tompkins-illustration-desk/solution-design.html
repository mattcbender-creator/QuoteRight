Design rules

What we hold to, whatever the back end becomes

  • Pick a scenario, not a product. The firm’s best case designer sets each preset once.
  • Start where advisors already are. The client record, Claude, or a phone.
  • Never make them wait or watch. They are told when it is ready.
  • Problems go to back office. A stalled run arrives later, not as an error.
  • Estimates are always labelled. Only carrier figures reach a client.
  • Every number has a source. The carrier PDF is attached, with date and version.

Why it holds up technically

Standard parts, arranged carefully

  • Carrier tools are forms that return a PDF. Filling a web form and reading a PDF are both well-established automation.
  • Logins sit in a secure vault on the firm’s system. Sessions are kept alive, and an advisor approves on their phone only if a carrier asks for a code.
  • A daily test case per carrier finds a changed screen before an advisor does.
  • Nothing is delivered unchecked. A mismatch between the request and the carrier PDF stops that carrier and alerts back office.
  • Full record. Who asked, when, which carrier software version, and the PDF itself.

What has to be true first

  • Carrier consent in writing for automated runs on the firm’s system. This is the largest dependency.
  • Compliance approval of the branded comparison and the labelled estimate.
  • A privacy decision on where client data is processed and stored.
  • A named owner for presets, carrier mappings and upkeep.
