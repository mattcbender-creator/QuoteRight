The client

A founder whose estate will owe more tax than it holds in cash

Jonathan is 52 and owns a manufacturing business through a holding company. Most of his $34.5 million net worth sits in private shares and real estate. On his death those assets are deemed sold, and the tax comes due whether or not anything has been sold.

What the numbers say

Observations

  1. Manulife projects the largest estate value. $26.83M at age 85 on the current scale, a 4.55% tax-free return on premiums, equivalent to 9.14% before tax inside the holding company.
  2. Sun Life builds accessible cash value fastest. $13.32M when premiums end in year 20, and cash value passes premiums paid in year 9. This matters if the policy may later be pledged as collateral.
  3. Manulife carries the lowest outlay. $455,900 a year, $0.47M less over 20 years than the highest premium.
  4. Canada Life holds up best if dividends fall. With the scale 1% lower, its age-85 death benefit keeps 79% of the current-scale value.

Where we would take this next

Run the carriers’ own illustrations at the client’s underwritten rate class, add a joint last-to-die version with Claire so the benefit lands when the tax does, and test the lead design against a 10-pay and a term-blended alternative before recommending a carrier.

Assumptions and important information

This is a sample. The client, the holding company and every figure in this document are fictional. The values were produced by a simplified projection model for demonstration; they were not generated by Canada Life, Manulife or Sun Life illustration software and are not quotes, offers or carrier-approved illustrations. Actual premiums, values and dividend scales will differ.

Basis. Male, age 52 nearest, non-smoker, standard risk, Ontario. $10,000,000 basic coverage, premiums payable annually for 20 years, dividend option paid-up additions, no riders, no policy loans or withdrawals. Product names are shown to indicate the type of plan each carrier offers in this category.

Dividends are not guaranteed. Current-scale values assume each carrier’s assumed dividend scale interest rate stays unchanged for the life of the policy. The alternate scale reduces that rate by one percentage point in all years. Guaranteed cash values assume no dividends are ever paid.

Tax. Estimated tax at death assumes Ontario top marginal rates, a 50% capital gains inclusion rate and nominal cost base on private shares. Pre-tax equivalent yield uses a 50.17% rate on corporate passive income. Capital dividend account credit is the death benefit less an estimated adjusted cost basis. This is not tax or legal advice.
